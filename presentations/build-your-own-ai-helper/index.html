The weekly scramble

Every week, a volunteer coordinator opens the sign-up spreadsheet and builds next week's schedule by hand.

Who's free Saturday? Who's done this before? Who got stuck with the phone bank last time?

5

Is this a good job for an AI helper?

  1. It happens again and again, like every week or month
  2. The information is digital and organized the same way every time (a spreadsheet, a form export or a website)
  3. The result looks the same every time

  4. A person was going to check it anyway

    Ours? Obviously.

Recipe card 1: the plan

ok so: every week our volunteer coordinator takes the sign-up spreadsheet and builds a shift calendar by hand. I want a small tool that drafts the calendar, and a person checks it before anyone is told.

Don't write code yet. Write docs/BUILD_PLAN.md. The calendar should keep every shift staffed with the right number of people, including an experienced person where needed; give people their preferred shifts first; never schedule anyone outside their availability or over their limit; and spread shifts fairly. Lock the decisions: runs on my laptop, reads signups.xlsx, makes a calendar file I can import into Google Calendar plus a one-page view to check, never emails or texts anyone. Break it into phases I can build one at a time.

In plain English: make a plan first. Don't build yet. A person checks it. It never contacts anyone.

Burnt batch 1: the missing people

The draft looked complete. Every shift full.

It had quietly left out 11 volunteers, including five experienced ones.

Their availability was typed in their own words, and the plan said a person has to confirm those first.

The lesson: ask what it left out.

20

Burnt batch 2: the Saturday problem

Every shift had an experienced person. Most had only one.

If that one person cancels, nobody on the shift has done it before.

hmmm. I think Saturday PM sessions ... people are more likely to drop that day for personal reasons. Let's get another experienced person in case they drop.

The AI didn't know Saturdays fall apart. The coordinator did.

The lesson: you know things the spreadsheet doesn't.

Recipe card 4: the instructions

Write docs/RUNBOOK.md for the volunteer coordinator who will run this every week. Assume they have never seen the code and never will. Start with the weekly review, then what to do when the sign-up form changes.

Written for the person who runs it next week. Or after you've moved on.

Where the human stays

  1. Read "Needs your attention" first
  2. Confirm anyone whose availability was typed in their own words
  3. Swap anyone you want. The tool re-checks every rule.
  4. Approve. Only then does the calendar file exist.
  5. Import it, and tell volunteers yourself
24

Disclose twice

In the tool's save history, for whoever maintains it

At the bottom of every schedule, for whoever reads it

Add a line at the bottom of every schedule saying it was drafted with AI from the sign-up form and checked by staff.
25

The checklist ✅

  1. Pick a task that repeats, uses information organized the same way every time, and has a result that looks the same each time. If not, write a checklist instead.
  2. Plan first. Lock the decisions. Write down what you're NOT building yet.
  3. One source. Add the second when the first is boring.
  4. Build with made-up data. Keep real personal information on your own computer.
  5. Ask the AI to check its own work, like a skeptic.
  6. Ask what it left out.
  7. Add what you know that the spreadsheet doesn't.
  8. Write the instructions for the next person.
  9. Decide where a person checks it, and make the tool stop there.

  10. Tell people AI helped. Twice.

Good fits at a nonprofit

  • A weekly list of new grant opportunities from funder websites
  • This month's program numbers, dropped into the same board-report table
  • A monthly check of your website for broken links
  • News coverage of your issue, gathered and summarized
Not a good fit: one-time judgment calls, or anything no one will check.
27

What can the AI see?

🟢 Public: your website, funder pages, published reports. Go ahead.

🟡 Internal: budgets, drafts, totals with no names. Check your organization's AI policy first.

🔴 Personal: donor, client or other personal information. Keep it out.

Rule of thumb: anything the AI reads leaves your building.
